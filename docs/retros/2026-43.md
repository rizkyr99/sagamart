## What shipped

- **Foundation:** docs, local stack, CI gates (single `ci` check, gitleaks), contracts tooling (buf, Redocly, ajv), branch protection.
- **Inventory (Go):**
  - reservations that never oversell (benchmark: 2,383 attempts/s, ADR-0008);
  - an expiry worker and outbox relay;
  - admin and browse calls that require the gateway's signed identity.
- **Catalog and carts (NestJS):** listing with keyset cursors, product pages, admin writes with versions and idempotency, and carts in DynamoDB with a 7-day TTL.
- **Gateway (NestJS):**
  - dev auth, signed internal identities and rate limiting;
  - browse and cart composition with live stock;
  - admin routes, with a circuit breaker and deadlines.
- **End-to-end flow test** in CI, including Inventory stopped, plus the browse benchmark.

## Keep

- **RFC and contracts before code.** RFC-0001 and RFC-0005 settled the hard parts (strict expiry, idempotency owned by the writer, one cart item) before any code. Contract checks in tests caught shape drift early.
- **Test-first, and proving the tests can fail.** Mutation checks (removing a version condition or a verification step) showed the race and identity tests were real.
- **Verifying on the live stack before calling something done.** It caught what unit tests couldn't: the gRPC cold-start `UNKNOWN`, real `ErrorInfo` decoding, and Redis failing open.
- **Automated review on every PR.** CodeRabbit found real bugs: a harness that leaked containers, the k6 request mix, "0 units reserved" stated without knowing it, and the fresh-checkout build.
- **Working AI-assisted, with you reviewing and merging.** Stage 1 was sized at about 55 h of work by its issue sizes and took about 12 h of your time. Your time goes to review, decisions and merges, not typing code.
- **Writing a miss up honestly.** The browse target (p99 < 200 ms) was missed, the report says why, and the fixes are tracked (#46–#49).
